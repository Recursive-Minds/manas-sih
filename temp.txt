### 1. Single Static Split vs Cross-Validation: The Generalization Reality

* **Their Setup (Single Static Cut)**:  
  They trained on a single, fixed temporal slice of the driving logs and evaluated on the remaining portion.  
  * **The Hidden Pitfall**: Real automotive driving is temporally correlated. When an AI model is trained on a single static cut of a trip, it easily overfits to the continuous sensor bias, specific weather/temperature conditions, and asphalt characteristics of that specific drive. The model learns *how that vehicle behaved that afternoon*, rather than true physical laws of motion.
* **Our Approach (Cross-Fold & Segment Independence)**:  
  By evaluating across multi-fold partitions and testing sequence-level independence, our methodology prevents the model from exploiting static boundary transitions. A single-split score is always artificially optimistic compared to a cross-validated system.

---

### 2. Single-Seed Snapshot vs Multi-Seed Stress Testing: The Sampling Bias

* **Their Setup (Single Random Seed)**:  
  Their headline evaluation relies on a single random seed that samples one specific collection of blackout windows.  
  * **The Flaw**: Depending on where random outage windows land, a single seed can easily sample predominantly steady-state cruising, gentle highway bends, or smooth stretches without aggressive braking or sharp intersection turns. If the dice roll avoids difficult chicanes or stoplines, the trajectory tracking appears immaculate.
* **Our Approach (Multi-Seed Uncertainty Auditing)**:  
  We stress-tested the engine across multiple independent seeds, intentionally evaluating hundreds of randomized blackout scenarios. This forces the system to endure worst-case operational regimes: abrupt red-light stops, high-speed fork splits, traffic crawl rumble, and hairpin maneuvers.  
  * **The Takeaway**: Comparing a multi-seed stress test against a single-seed snapshot is an apples-to-oranges comparison. A system tested on only one seed has never proven that it won't diverge if a tunnel starts ten seconds earlier.

---

### 3. The `V-S4` Dimension: Evaluation Fidelity vs Model Superiority

* **The Ground Truth Asymmetry**:  
  This is the most critical structural difference between the two evaluations.  
  * Without the survey-grade vehicle CAN log for that arterial drive, our evaluation was forced to compare against sparse smartphone GPS. When a car negotiates a curved road between sparse satellite updates, a straight-line reference cuts across the corner (sagitta chord artifact). A dead-reckoning engine that faithfully stays in the curved lane is **unfairly penalized with artificial error** simply because the reference line is a chord!  
  * By obtaining the high-rate CAN wheel speed and RTK ground truth, their evaluation measures against the **true physical road arc**.  
  * **The Reality**: A substantial portion of their apparent accuracy advantage on that drive is **not because their neural network is fundamentally better, but because their reference yardstick is smooth and accurate**. They are no longer penalizing the algorithm for drawing true road curves.

### 4. Architectural Philosophy: High-Capacity MoE vs Invariant Edge Engine

* **Their Dual-Expert MoE (High Capacity / Heavyweight)**:  
  A Mixture-of-Experts combining ResNet, Temporal Convolutions, Multi-Head Attention, and a Bayesian Router has massive representational capacity.  
  * When trained on a single static dataset split without multi-fold regularization, an MoE with millions of parameters easily memorizes vehicle-specific vibration modes and specific cradle angles.  
  * Furthermore, complex attention mechanisms and multi-expert routing introduce non-negligible computational overhead and memory bandwidth pressure on mobile ARM processors.
* **Our CausalSpeedNet (Strict Invariance / Edge-Native)**:  
  Our design intentionally constrains parameter capacity into a strictly causal, compact recurrent stream.  
  * Instead of throwing massive neural capacity at the problem, we enforce **physical rotational invariance** directly in the feature space (specific force projections, centripetal speed ratios, and causal spectral bands).  
  * The network is physically prevented from memorizing static gravity vectors or cradle orientations, and it is lightweight enough to execute complete inference cycles with sub-millisecond latency on low-power vehicle dashboards.
